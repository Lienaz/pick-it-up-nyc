Pick It Up, New York

New York was the first big U.S. city to pass a pooper scooper law, in 1978. Dog waste complaints are now at their highest level since at least 2010, and the law is almost never enforced.

Complaints this year 3,307

dog waste complaints to 311 from January through September 2026, the most for that stretch since at least 2010.

2.8 times the 2021 total for the same months (1,174). Includes about 1,000 extra complaints during the February snowmelt; without February, 2026 is still the highest since 2011.

Tickets written 2

summonses for failing to pick up after a dog in all of 2025, according to the Department of Sanitation. 311 logged 2,660 complaints that year.

In the law's first year, the city wrote 1,616 summonses, after a three-month grace period with 7,000 warnings, according to a 1979 press release from Mayor Ed Koch.

A decade of decline, then a steady climb

Complaints fell to a pandemic low in 2020–21, then rose every year. February 2026 alone brought 1,230, when snow from the Jan. 25 storm melted and exposed weeks of waste.

Seasonal pattern, 2010–2025

February is complaint season, snow or no snow

Across 2010–2025, February ran about 55% above an average month, and the bump appears even in nearly snowless winters. Long stretches of snow cover make it much worse.

The law

New York was the first big city with a pooper scooper law

1930s–78
"Curb Your Dog" signs ask owners to steer dogs into the gutter. The rule is widely ignored.
1978
Mayor Ed Koch takes the measure to Albany after the City Council won't act. The Canine Waste Law (Public Health Law §1310) takes effect Aug. 1. Activist Fran Lee campaigned for it; the ASPCA opposed it.
Today
Fine of up to $250, enforced by Sanitation, but an agent has to see the owner walk away.
2026
The City Council takes up the SCOOP Act package: bag dispensers, a composting pilot, an education campaign and required inspections after repeated complaints on a block.
Why it doesn't work 93–97%

of dog waste complaints each year since 2022 are closed with "no violation" or "no condition" found. By the time an inspector arrives, the dog and the owner are long gone.
